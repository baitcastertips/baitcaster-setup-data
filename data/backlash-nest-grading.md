# Grading a bird's nest: light, buried, severe

Most tangles people cut are not severe. Grading the nest first is what decides which
method you use, and it is the step that saves line.

## Light

A loose pile of loops sitting on top of the spool. The line still feeds. This usually
comes out in seconds.

## Buried

The loops are pulled down into the spool and the spool looks swollen. The line will not
feed by hand.

## Severe

The line is compressed and crossed under real tension, often with the lure still out and
a belly of slack line on the water. This is the one people cut — and usually
unnecessarily.

## The spool-off method (for a buried or severe nest)

1. Loosen the drag to zero.
2. Hold the spool with your thumb and pull a few yards of line off the **side of the
   spool** — not off the level-wind, off the spool face.
3. Let that freed line lie on the deck or in the water, untangled.
4. Reach into the nest and find the loop that is now exposed.
5. Repeat until the pile releases.

## What makes a backlash worse

- Pulling the line tight against a locked spool. This buries the loops instead of freeing
  them.
- Reeling to "wind it out." Reeling drives line over the crossed loops and locks the nest.
- Picking at the middle of the pile instead of the outside loop.
- Cutting before you have tried the spool-off method.
- Re-spooling the freed line loose. Loose line on the spool causes the next backlash.

## Quick reference

- Thumb the spool. Stop reeling.
- Grade the nest: light, buried, or severe.
- Drag to zero. Pull line off against a thumbed spool.
- Free the outside loop first, one at a time.
- Buried nest: pull line off the spool face, not the level-wind.
- Rewind under light thumb pressure so it lays flat.
- Cut only if the line is fused or frayed, then check the line above the cut.
